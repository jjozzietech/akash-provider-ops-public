## // what shipped

**The provider network was rebuilt as a routed L3 fabric.**

The provider ran on a single-core legacy L2 switching model. It now runs on a
dual-TOR Nexus fabric with vPC and HSRP, dedicated service VLANs, and routed
northbound connectivity. Hypervisor hosts and Akash worker nodes are dual-homed
or directly attached to the new fabric. Management traffic is separated onto its
own VLAN, provider traffic onto another.

Three reasons for doing it, in order of weight. The single-core design was a
single point of failure for the entire provider — one switch failure took
everything. Service isolation was by convention rather than by topology. And the
old design made maintenance all-or-nothing: any change to the core meant taking
the provider offline, which is why several deferred improvements stayed deferred.
The new fabric allows a TOR to be taken out of service without the provider
going with it.

Completed 20 September, so this report is written four days into it. It is done,
not settled — the interesting failure modes of a new fabric surface over weeks,
not days, and anything that turns up will be in report #3.

**Rack migration finished.**

The physical relocation that began in August is complete across both phases. No
data loss, no lease loss across the full sequence. The migration spare node that
absorbed the drain remains in the cluster, cordoned, in case a future move needs
it.

**Silent-partition detection is live.**

Report #1 described a node that stayed `Ready` to the API server for roughly two
weeks while having no working network path — kubelet posting status fine,
Calico down, nothing schedulable, no alert. The detection for it now runs in the
daily health check: Ceph OSD status cross-referenced against Kubernetes node-Ready
state, where divergence flags a partition candidate.

It reports its own coverage honestly, which matters more than the check itself:
**19 of 24 nodes**. Five nodes carry no Ceph OSD, so there is nothing to
cross-reference against, and those five are invisible to it. That is the same gap
disclosed when the mitigation was announced, still open. Outbound heartbeat per
node is the follow-up that closes it and it is not built yet.

**Anti-mining.** One eviction this month, 13 September. No active miner workloads
at month end. The community blacklist stands at 37 entries.

## // what broke

**Four days of zero bidding during the fabric cutover.**

18–21 September, the provider created no bids. The window overlaps the Phase 3
fabric cutover exactly. It recovered on its own once the network stabilised, with
no intervention and no lease loss — active leases kept running throughout; only
new bidding stopped.

Filed here rather than under the migration because of what it says about
monitoring rather than about the network. A provider that stops bidding looks
identical, from every dashboard, to a provider that simply isn't winning. Nothing
alerted. It was found afterwards by counting transactions, which is the same
class of problem as the silent partition: a failure whose signature is an
*absence*, and absences do not generate log lines.

**The provider cannot match orders that require an audit attestation.**

The attestation was removed on 18 August in an auditor scope change. The practical
consequence is sharper than expected, and it took an outside dataset to make it
visible.

A tenant running automated benchmark deployments noticed the provider had stopped
bidding on their orders and raised it publicly, with their own data: hundreds of
orders broadcast, hundreds of bids drawn from other providers, none from here —
while their market snapshots showed this provider advertising available in 100% of
over a thousand samples across the same window.

Pulling one of their live orders off-chain gave the answer. It carries a
`requirements.signed_by.any_of` constraint naming a specific auditor. All twelve
of their open orders carry it. Without that attestation the order is unmatchable
at the chain level, and the bid engine correctly declines — every time, silently,
because a declined bid writes nothing anywhere.

The latest daily health check shows the shape of it: **75 orders seen in 24 hours,
75 declined, 0 wins.**

Two things worth separating. The mechanism is working as designed — this is not a
bug in the bid engine, the chain, or the tenant's tooling. And the effect is not
limited to one tenant: any order carrying that requirement is invisible in the same
way. Console's default deployment flow filters to audited providers, so the
population affected is not small.

A re-audit request went out on 21 August. No response yet. That is the whole of the
remediation available, and it is not in this operator's hands.

**Uptime instrumentation didn't happen.** Covered above. Promised for September,
delivered in neither September nor a reason until now.

the pilot dataset against local records surfaced a field empty in every row of it,

from the chain rather than from either side's tooling. A failure attributed here

correlation rather than causation, and two genuine provider-side failures the
dataset had missed were volunteered from this end with their on-chain references.
